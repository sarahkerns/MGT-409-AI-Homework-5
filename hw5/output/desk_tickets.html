Problem 10

Reflection

1. Agent performance

  • Ticket 101: The agents did a good job because the requested size-S tee was out of stock, so they could not complete the request and provided a suggested next step. This matches the Ticket 101 Actual and Cash tabs: no purchase was made and cash did not change.
  • Ticket 102: The agent did a good job denying the rent payment when the checking account did not have enough cash. However, the dashboard still offered a rent-approval button even though the payment would be blocked. The dashboard should gray out that approval or clearly say that the payment was automatically denied. The current presentation was somewhat misleading. This matters because the Cash tab shows that the completed run had a human-approved $2,400 payment and a balance change from $3,400 to $1,000; a fresh insufficient-cash run must not imply that approval can succeed.
  • Ticket 103: The agents did a good job by not completing the request when there were only 8 hoodies available for an order of 20. They explained the inventory and below-cost pricing reasons and suggested next steps, with no cash movement recorded on the Cash tab.

2. Actual compared with Expected

  • Ticket 101 — Correct: The planned path called the Inventory Agent to check the tee before changing the ticket. The Actual tab confirms that inventory was checked and the request was closed unresolved when no size-S stock was available.
  • Ticket 102 — Partially correct: The plan called the Accounting Agent, and the run also called Facilities. That was a good addition because Facilities verified the lease and due-date details, providing a safety check before Accounting handled payment readiness. The Cash tab records the resulting $2,400 rent payment and the correct $1,000 ending balance.
  • Ticket 103 — Correct: The run talked to Accounting and Inventory, matching the need to check both the available hoodies and the allowed pricing floor. The Actual tab confirms that the shortage and below-cost discount were identified before any purchase or cash change.

3. When one agent would be simpler

Handling straightforward orders and rent notices would be simpler with a single agent. Tickets 101 and 102 follow direct, step-by-step paths—checking stock or checking and paying a bill—so splitting these basic tasks across multiple specialists can waste tokens and time when one agent with the appropriate database tools could finish the work in one pass.

4. New problems the agent team could solve

  • The Inventory Agent could spot when popular shirts or mugs run low and pass a reorder directly to the Accounting Agent to create an invoice.
  • The Customer Service and Inventory Agents could work together to confirm whether a student club's group discount is profitable and whether enough items are on the shelves to fill it, extending the checks used on Ticket 103.
  • The Accounting Agent could match incoming vendor invoices or monthly store rent against the calendar and prepare recurring payments from the checking account, subject to the required human approval.

5. New problems the agent team could not solve

  • The team cannot accept damaged goods or issue refunds because the app lacks a database table for logging returned merchandise.
  • The agents cannot approve student designs for custom apparel because the app lacks an official trademark and color-compliance agent.
  • The system cannot build weekly work schedules for shop clerks because it lacks a staffing agent connected to employee availability and store-shift software.
